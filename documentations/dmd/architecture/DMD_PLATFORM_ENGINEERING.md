# DMD — Platform Engineering

**Authority:** canonical

**Purpose:** Define when a client engagement becomes platform engineering rather than standard delivery or ordinary custom product development.

## 1. Definition

**Platform Engineering** is the highest technical delivery class in the DMD commercial model.

It applies when the client is no longer asking only for a website, a single application, or a set of custom features, but for a broader software system that requires reusable platform architecture, advanced workflow automation, internal engines, orchestration, AI workflows, multi-role administration, or a private/extensible product foundation.

Internal classification:

```text
PLATFORM_ENGINEERING
```

Client-facing language may remain simpler:

```text
Private Platform
Platform Development
Advanced Software Platform
Custom Business Platform
```

The internal technical term does not have to be exposed to the client unless it improves clarity.

## 2. Position in the DMD delivery model

DMD distinguishes three primary delivery classes:

```text
PRODUCTIZED DELIVERY
→ existing capabilities
→ configuration
→ design
→ limited extensions

CUSTOM PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT
→ reusable foundation
→ custom workflows
→ integrations
→ domain-specific logic
→ substantial custom development

PLATFORM ENGINEERING
→ platform architecture
→ reusable engines
→ orchestration
→ automation
→ AI workflows
→ advanced operational systems
→ private/extensible product foundation
```

Platform Engineering is not simply “Custom Product with more features.” The difference is architectural.

A Custom Product may solve one client's workflow very well. A Platform Engineering engagement creates a system intended to support broader reuse, extensibility, multiple workflows, multiple teams, multiple client types, future product evolution, or a substantial internal operational model.

## 3. Typical signals

DMD agents may classify an opportunity as a Platform Engineering candidate when the client requests several of the following:

```text
multi-tenant architecture
multiple organizations or workspaces
complex roles and permissions
reusable workflow engines
approval orchestration
automation infrastructure
AI orchestration
AI agents
project intelligence
product intelligence
event-driven workflows
advanced auditability
multi-stage client journeys
custom admin control center
custom client portal
billing/subscription infrastructure
complex provider integrations
internal operational tooling
private product variant
ability to resell the system to their own customers
```

No single feature automatically makes a project Platform Engineering. The classification depends on the total architecture, reuse requirements, operational responsibility, future extensibility, and business model.

## 4. Client intent before technology

The client should not have to understand platform-engineering terminology.

A client may say:

> We need an application where our customers log in, submit requests, AI analyzes them, our team approves them, work is assigned automatically, the customer sees progress, and later we want to sell the same system to other companies.

DMD should interpret the underlying requirements:

```text
customer accounts
request intake
AI interpretation
approval workflow
work orchestration
project tracking
notifications
admin control
multi-client reuse
future commercialization
```

The system may then classify the opportunity internally as a Platform Engineering candidate.

The client-facing response should remain business-oriented:

> What you are describing is closer to a private software platform than a standard website or single-purpose application. We can build it in phases and decide how much automation and platform infrastructure should be included in the first release.

## 5. Progressive commercial discovery

DMD should not immediately present the largest possible offer.

Recommended flow:

```text
client intent
→ capability analysis
→ architecture complexity
→ platform signals
→ explain available direction
→ confirm interest
→ present approximate scope, price and timeline
→ deeper discovery only if commercially viable
```

The agent may first explain that the requested system can be delivered in more than one way.

Example:

> We can approach this as a custom product focused only on your current workflow, or as a broader private platform designed for future expansion, automation and multiple teams or customers.

Only after the client confirms interest should the system expand the discussion.

## 6. Useful additional capabilities

A sales or discovery agent may suggest capabilities the client has not explicitly requested when they are a logical consequence of the stated business goal.

Examples:

```text
roles and permissions
approval workflows
audit trail
notifications
email infrastructure
customer portal
admin control center
analytics
billing
multi-tenant support
AI workflow automation
document management
project tracking
integration layer
```

These must not be presented as mandatory merely because they exist.

Preferred client language:

> If you expect several teams to use the system, or plan to offer it to your own customers later, we should also consider roles, permissions, audit history and multi-organization support. They do not all need to be part of the first phase.

The objective is informed discovery, not aggressive upselling.

## 7. Commercial qualification gate

Before detailed architecture work begins, DMD should confirm that the client understands the approximate commercial class.

Example:

```text
Estimated delivery:
24–28 weeks

Estimated engineering effort:
X–Y hours

Commercial basis:
engineering rate × estimated work
+ integrations
+ infrastructure
+ delivery risk
+ platform complexity
```

At this stage the number is still an estimate, not a final proposal.

If the client confirms that the range is acceptable, DMD continues into deeper Discovery and Solution Blueprint work.

If the range is too high, the system should offer a simpler route rather than ending the conversation.

## 8. Controlled downsell

A Platform Engineering opportunity may be reduced to Custom Product Development:

```text
PLATFORM ENGINEERING
        ↓ budget too high
CUSTOM PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT
```

Client-facing explanation:

> We can reduce the scope significantly. You would not receive the complete platform infrastructure or our broader reusable engineering layers, but we can build a custom product around the workflows that are most important to your business today.

The reduced Custom Product may still include:

```text
custom frontend
admin panel
client accounts
CMS
workflow management
email
notifications
selected integrations
selected AI capabilities
```

while excluding:

```text
general-purpose reusable engines
full orchestration layer
broad multi-tenant platform infrastructure
full Product Intelligence
full Project Intelligence
technology licensing
```

This preserves a valid path without promising the full platform at an unrealistic price.

## 9. Platform Engineering is not Technology Licensing

This distinction is mandatory.

A client may pay for a large Platform Engineering engagement without obtaining ownership of DMDevelon core technology.

Platform Engineering answers:

> What are we building?

Technology Licensing answers:

> What rights does the client receive over the technology?

These are separate commercial decisions.

A private platform may use DMDevelon reusable engines, libraries, architecture, orchestration or know-how while those components remain DMDevelon intellectual property.

The client receives only the rights defined in the applicable agreement.

## 10. Core IP boundary

Unless explicitly agreed otherwise, Platform Engineering does not transfer ownership of:

```text
DMDevelon reusable engines
general-purpose orchestration
shared automation infrastructure
Product Intelligence
Project Intelligence
generic AI workflow infrastructure
internal development tooling
shared libraries
pre-existing platform architecture
methods and know-how
```

Client-specific code, configuration, data, branding, content and custom business logic may be treated separately according to the agreed rights model.

The contract must define the boundary.

## 11. Pricing principle

Platform Engineering is priced according to more than feature count.

Relevant factors include:

```text
engineering effort
architecture complexity
integration complexity
delivery risk
security requirements
reusability
operational responsibility
maintenance burden
infrastructure
AI usage
provider dependencies
documentation
deployment
support
IP exposure
```

The core principle is:

```text
WE DO NOT PRICE ONLY FEATURES.
WE PRICE THE SYSTEM CLASS, DELIVERY RESPONSIBILITY AND RIGHTS.
```

## 12. DMD routing rule

DMD should classify the solution internally as:

```text
PRODUCTIZED_DELIVERY
CUSTOM_PRODUCT
PLATFORM_ENGINEERING
```

The classification is determined from:

```text
business goal
required capabilities
existing product fit
custom development delta
architecture complexity
reuse requirement
operational responsibility
future commercialization
maintenance burden
```

AI may interpret the request and propose a classification. The system decides the commercial route.

Human review remains required for large Platform Engineering engagements before price, scope or legal commitments become binding.

## 14. Validation principle

Platform Engineering rules should be validated through real delivery work.

DMD should record:

```text
what the client requested
what DMD classified
what was recommended
what was actually sold
actual effort
actual delivery duration
scope changes
maintenance burden
commercial outcome
```

This evidence will later improve pricing, qualification and Product/Demand Intelligence.

## 15. Summary

Platform Engineering is appropriate when the engagement becomes the development of a private or extensible software platform rather than a conventional site or custom application.

It may include:

```text
platform architecture
reusable engines
orchestration
automation
AI workflows
advanced operational systems
multi-tenant or multi-team support
private product variants
```

It does **not** automatically include ownership or licensing of DMDevelon core technology.

Technical delivery class and intellectual-property rights remain separate decisions.
